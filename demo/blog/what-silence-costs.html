What silence costs a voice agent

Most of a conversation is not the user talking. It is the agent talking, the model thinking, and pauses. Every API that bills by audio duration bills for all of it, because the decision about what was speech is made on the server, after the audio has already crossed the network and entered the meter.

Three line items

Audio in. Streaming speech-to-text is priced per minute of audio sent (Deepgram Nova-3 streaming is listed around $0.0048 per minute by third-party summaries; OpenAI's realtime models bill audio input per token, about $32 per million). If the user speaks a third of the time, two thirds of that bill is silence and the agent's own voice leaking into the microphone. A detector on the device that forwards only confirmed speech, with a 300 ms pre-roll, cuts the billed input to the user's talk time.

Audio out. Text-to-speech is billed per character generated, not per character heard. An agent that writes a paragraph and gets interrupted after the first sentence paid for the paragraph. Auricle synthesises clause by clause and stops the chain at the interruption, so an interrupted reply costs the clauses that played.

Context. If the user interrupts and the full unheard reply stays in the transcript, every later prompt carries it. Auricle counts played samples in the playback worklet, maps them back to text, and cuts the assistant message to what was actually heard. Smaller prompts, and the model continues the conversation the user had rather than the one it wrote.

The arithmetic, with your numbers

The demo prints a session readout: minutes of audio sent to speech-to-text versus session length, characters synthesised versus characters avoided by interruption, median stop latency. Run a conversation and read it off. Rates change monthly and differ by vendor, so we do not put a dollar figure on the landing page; we put the ratio, measured, and leave the vendor rate table to the vendors' own pricing pages, which change monthly.

Why the platforms do not do this

A hosted voice platform bills per minute of media it carries. Moving the listener to the client removes the minute. It is not that they cannot; it is that their price list is the reason not to. An open-source listener that runs next to the microphone has no such reason, which is why this is a library and not a service.
